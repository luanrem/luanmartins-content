The cheapest server I run costs nothing, and it has been up longer than some
things I have been paid to maintain. That is less impressive than it sounds —
and more interesting than it sounds, for a reason that took me a while to
notice.

## What free actually means

A free-tier ARM instance gives you a real machine: a handful of cores, a few
gigabytes of memory, a persistent disk, a public address. What it does not give
you is headroom. There is no burst capacity to hide behind, no autoscaling to
paper over a bad query, and no support contract when it goes sideways.

That sounds like a list of disadvantages. In practice it is a list of things
that would have let me avoid decisions.

## The constraint that helped

Hermes classifies job listings with an LLM. The obvious architecture keeps a
model resident and pipes work to it. On this box that is simply not available —
the memory is not there. So classification became a per-item API call, which
forced two things I would otherwise have skipped:

**Deduplication had to come first.** If every call costs money, paying twice for
the same listing under two different titles becomes visible immediately. On a
machine with headroom, that waste would have hidden inside a batch job for
months.

**The unit of work had to be small.** A per-item call fails per item. A resident
model processing a batch fails as a batch, and then you are rerunning
twenty minutes of work to fix one bad record.

Neither of these is a clever idea. They are both things I know to do and would
not have bothered doing, because nothing would have punished me for skipping
them.

## What it takes away

I want to be honest about the other side, because "constraints are good" is the
kind of thing that is true right up until it is a rationalization.

Rebuilds are slow. Anything that would normally be a parallel build is serial
here, and I feel it every time. There is no failover: when the instance goes
down, the pipeline is down, and the only reason that is acceptable is that
nobody but me depends on it. And I have spent evenings tuning memory limits that
would have been a dropdown on a paid tier — time that bought me nothing except
the machine staying up.

## The part that generalizes

The lesson is not "use free hardware". It is that **a budget you cannot exceed
turns architecture decisions into forced choices**, and forced choices get made.
On a generous machine, the same decisions stay open forever, because nothing
ever makes you close them.
